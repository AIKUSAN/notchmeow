# CC0 cat sound candidates, fifth set (sub-project C)

Researched 2026-10-05. The research itself downloaded no audio and listened to nothing: the pages were read over plain HTTP (no login, no API key, no account), and a HEAD request read each preview's size. The owner then approved downloading the 24 previews, and **every window below was verified from the decoded audio on 2026-10-05** (see the next section); nobody has listened to them yet.

## Method

- Searched Freesound's public search pages with the Creative Commons 0 filter and "group by pack" off, so every clip of an uploader shows. Also read uploader and pack listings, BigSoundBank's cat search and OpenGameArt's CC0 sound search.
- Excluded every clip in `cc0-candidates.md`, in `.claude/sounds/candidates/manifest.json` and in the four set files. A grep confirms none of the 24 chosen IDs appears in any of them.
- **Licence:** read on each chosen sound's own page. The licence box says Creative Commons 0 (link `creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/`) on all 24 files, checked 2026-10-05.
- **Fit and timestamps:** from the uploader's description and the waveform and spectrogram images on each sound's page (images only, not audio). The images show the noise floor, where the voice sits in time and the pitch contour. **Those first windows were estimates.** They were then checked against the real audio (verification below); each row shows the verified window and the estimate it replaced.

## The voice: Tabby, by foxboyprower

One animal, one uploader: Tabby, a domestic tabby cat recorded in March 2020 (the uploader's pack "Old Tabby Sounds"). She was 17 or 18 when recorded (the uploader gives her years as 2002 to 2020) and has since died. All 66 of the uploader's Tabby clips are CC0: 21 mews, 17 squeaks, 5 exhales, 14 purrs and some feeding noise, with the uploader's own sound-quality and volume ratings in each description. The clips are 44.1 kHz, 24-bit stereo WAV and mostly 0.3 to 0.8 s long, so each is already a single sound with no editing needed.

Why her:
- **Coverage.** No other CC0 uploader has this many separate, short, labelled vocalisations of one cat. She covers 13 of the 15 moments with her own voice, and every one of the 13 has a distinct clip. Nine moments also get a Tabby backup.
- **Matches the slot table.** The uploader says one squeak is the noise she made when poked while resting: that is the slap and annoyed pokes. A clip labelled as a question ("rawr?") rises at the end. A "soft mew" suits love.
- **Different from the four sets.** Emi, Steff's male cat, Oscar the kitten and the Best of mix are all adult or kitten meows and trills. Tabby is a thin, slightly croaky older cat, so the comparison is a real one.

Runners-up that were not chosen, and why:
- **sokworks, the cat Sugar** (pack "Assorted Cat Noises", Freesound 866529 to 866536, 48 kHz, 16-bit, Zoom H1essential). The cleanest recordings found, and the uploader labels each by mood (squeaky request, resigned, exasperated, gentle, appeal). But there are only 8 meows, so 13 moments would reuse them heavily, and the purr in that pack is a second cat (Chimi). A good swap if Tabby's noise is a problem.
- **PuzzlingGGG** (22 meows, Freesound 859713 to 859734): a strong room-noise floor in every spectrogram, phone recording.
- **Sadiquecat's Perran:** the best quality (192 kHz) and a wide range, but the uploader already feeds Steff's set and Best of, and the long recordings would need timestamps I cannot verify without listening.
- **Other uploaders** (fthgurdy, huw2k8, aunrea, TheKingOfGeeks360): fewer than six clips each, or one mood only.

Two moments borrow, from two other uploaders: **search** (and a dizzy backup) from jsbarrett, **sleep** from lolamadeus.

## Verification from the audio (2026-10-05)

Each preview mp3 was decoded with the repo's `AfconvertLoader` (48 kHz mono) and measured with the repo's DSP helpers (`highpass`, `rms`, `lin_to_db`):

- **Where the voice is.** A 30 ms RMS envelope of the signal high-passed at 100 to 150 Hz (the build high-passes at 100 Hz), read against each clip's own noise floor and against a pitch-periodicity test (normalised autocorrelation, 150 to 1500 Hz) that tells a voiced mew from room noise. The chosen window starts where the level reaches 18 dB under the voice's peak and ends where it falls back below that, plus a few ms: about 15 ms of margin each side, which the build's 3 ms fade-in and 20 ms fade-out sit inside.
- **Clicks.** For each window, the largest sample-to-sample jump in its last 40 ms against the window's 99th-percentile jump. Backups with a transient above 3 times (squeak16 at 0.331 s, mew17 at 0.292 s, squeak12 at 0.343 s) were cut before it.
- **The two long clips.** Both have clean silence around the sounds, so one sound cuts out whole: the snore (7.65 to 8.64 s) and the search trill (9.84 to 10.21 s) are each a complete event, not a slice of a longer one. All eight trills and ten snores were listed this way (see their rows).
- **Moved more than 30 ms** from the estimate (all marked MOVED in the table). Primaries: sleep start 7.60 to 7.65 and end 8.75 to 8.64; search start 9.65 to 9.84 and end 10.45 to 10.21; annoyed end 0.42 to 0.38; dizzy start 0.08 to 0.12; slap end 0.84 to 0.80. Backups: greet squeak11 end 0.36 to 0.325; slap squeak04 start 0.20 to 0.25; error mew17 end 0.36 to 0.285; proud squeak12 end 0.40 to 0.335; wink squeak10 end 0.20 to 0.15; think mew03 end 0.40 to 0.44; the jsbarrett dizzy backup start 7.50 to 7.69 and end 8.40 to 8.33; the search backup start 3.30 to 3.43 and end 4.20 to 4.14. The other windows moved 30 ms or less.
- **Level lift the build needs.** Tabby's clips were recorded quietly, so the build raises them a lot to reach -20 LUFS: greet +35.2 dB, error +28.7, slap +21.6, love +20.5, rate +20.3, wink +18.5, approval +18.4, think +17.8, annoyed +17.6, dizzy +15.6, finish +12.2, proud +10.3, question +6.1, search +6.1, sleep +4.8 (the Kitten set, for comparison, is cut by 2 to 8 dB). The noise floor rises with them: expect audible hiss on greet, error and slap, and listen for it first. No limiter action and all 15 meet their targets.
- **Weak clips.** wink squeak10 (backup) peaks at -53 dB and is only 0.13 s long; slap squeak04 (backup) peaks at -46 dB. Neither is in the recipe. The primary with the weakest voice-to-noise ratio is error mew06 (the voice is about 7 dB over its floor in the plain envelope, about 18 dB once high-passed at 150 Hz); see the build report for how it comes out.

## Gaps and risks

- **Voice quality.** Tabby was recorded on a consumer USB microphone, and the uploader's own ratings are modest (quality 2 to 5 out of 5, mostly 3). The best clips chosen look clean in the spectrograms (dark backgrounds, clear harmonics), but a mild hiss floor is likely. The build's loudness lift will raise it for the quiet picks (greet, love, slap, error): listen for hiss first.
- **Short voice.** Her sounds are about 0.2 to 0.4 s of actual voice, so annoyed, error and rate are brief blips next to the 0.7 to 1.0 s of the other sets. That suits slap and wink, but not the upset moments.
- **No purr, no trill, no chatter from Tabby.**
  - Her 14 purrs are quiet in the waveform and sit almost entirely in the bass, which the build's 100 Hz high-pass removes. So **love has no purr and no backup**, only the soft mew.
  - **Search is a trill, not chatter.** The only real chatter found is CatPhony's 8 kHz mono MP3 phone clips, which the brief calls fallback-only. The other sets use made-in-code 12 Hz chatter. Keeping that for the fifth set is a fair choice.
  - **Sleep is a snore from another cat.** A snore carries less identity than a meow, but the mixed-voices risk still applies to it, to search and to the dizzy backup.
